Mainnet

SWARM mainnet is live.

SWARM mainnet has been live since 2 October 2026 at 15:42 UTC. The genesis block holds no spendable coins; every SWM that exists has been mined since that moment. The start is closed for 30 days. Until 31 October 2026, 15:42 UTC only the project’s own machines can mine: in those 29 days about 33,408 blocks are produced, about 208,800 SWM (80% to the project’s mining wallet, 20% to the three funds), about 0.99% of the cap. From then until 1 November 2026, 15:42 UTC the people on the waiting list can mine too; from that moment mining is open to everyone and the node software will be published on this site. Why: the infrastructure around the coin, liquidity pools included, needs coins before public mining begins; the aim is an orderly, predictable start.

The coin

Ticker SWM. 6.25 SWM per block, a block every 75 seconds, halving every 1,680,000 blocks (about four years), 20,999,987.3152 SWM at most. Mining rewards mature after 100 blocks.

The work

Proof of work, Equihash 200,9, inherited unchanged. Until 31 October 2026, 15:42 UTC only the project’s own machines mine, then for 24 hours the people on the waiting list too; from 1 November 2026, 15:42 UTC anyone can. No checkpoints, no admin key, no way to mint a coin without finding a block.

Live

The split

80% of every block goes to the miner who found it. The other 20% is paid block by block to the three project funds — 8% Core Development, 4% Grants & Ecosystem, 8% Community & Development Reserve — at the published addresses below. Fixed in the genesis rules; it is not a premine and it cannot be changed without a protocol upgrade.

What SWARM is

A currency, a network, and the software to run both.

SWARM is a privacy coin: a new cryptocurrency, ticker SWM, with its own blockchain. It is secured by proof of work, its supply is fixed in the code, and every payment can be shielded so that sender, receiver and amount stay encrypted on-chain. It is built on proven, open-source code that has been securing real money for years, with the consensus rules and cryptography left exactly as they are.

SWARM in plain facts, on one page

The money

6.25 SWM per block, a block every 75 seconds, a halving every 1,680,000 blocks and a ceiling of 20,999,987.3152 SWM. The genesis block holds no coins, so every coin is mined; until 31 October 2026, 15:42 UTC only the project’s own machines mine, and public mining opens on 1 November 2026, 15:42 UTC. Of each block reward 80% goes to the miner and 20% to three published project destinations, block by block, for the life of the chain.

The software

SWARM Wallet holds, sends and receives, shielded or transparent, on desktop and phone. A public block explorer for mainnet is live. All of it is open source: read it, build it, check it.

The people

A founding team builds the software in the open. Until 31 October 2026, 15:42 UTC the project alone mines; the people on the waiting list follow 24 hours before public mining opens on 1 November 2026, 15:42 UTC, and from then on anyone can run a node and mine, with no account to apply for. There is no admin key, and the rules every node enforces are the same for the founders as for anyone else.

The privacy

Shielded transactions use zero-knowledge proofs, so the chain records that a valid payment happened without recording who paid whom or how much. Transparent payments exist too, and every wallet says plainly which kind you are about to make.

The mining

Proof of work is Equihash, with a proven per-block difficulty rule left unchanged. For the first 29 days after launch only the project’s own machines mine, then for 24 hours the people on the waiting list too; from 1 November 2026, 15:42 UTC mining is open to everyone. As the network grows, difficulty rises and larger miners can join; nothing in the rules keeps anyone out.

The principles

Reuse proven code; never reinvent consensus or cryptography. Nothing runs on your machine without your consent. No hidden treasury: the genesis block holds no coins, every allocation is visible in every block, and the closed start is stated in full, with its dates and amounts.

The network, live

SWARM mainnet is running: blocks every 75 seconds, 6.25 SWM each, the 80 / 8 / 4 / 8 split paid in every one of them. It has been live since 2 October 2026, and its genesis block holds no coins; the genesis hash and the three destination addresses are on the network page so anyone can check they are on the real chain.

What comes next

The coin and the network are the foundation. On top of them: SWARM Market, an online marketplace where you pay merchants in SWM; a privacy browser with the wallet built in; and private messaging, end-to-end encrypted, with payments inside the conversation. The first SWARM Browser pre-release for Windows is already in the Ecosystem.

The Hive

A hive is just a lot of small jobs, done honestly.

SWARM borrows its shape from bees, because a beehive is the clearest picture of what a peer-to-peer network actually is: no boss, no central server, thousands of small contributions adding up to something none of them could manage alone.

Bees

Everyone running a node. Each one keeps its own copy of the rules and checks every block for itself, so nobody has to be trusted.

Foragers

The miners. They spend real electricity on proof of work to secure the chain. The work is Equihash. Until 31 October 2026, 15:42 UTC only the project’s own machines forage, then for 24 hours the people on the waiting list too; from 1 November 2026, 15:42 UTC anyone can, and nothing in the rules keeps bigger miners out.

Honey

The block reward. 6.25 SWM for every block found, paid for real work — never minted for anyone in advance.

Honeycomb

The blockchain. One cell for each block, sealed in order, each one referring back to the last — a shared ledger anyone can re-check.

The Hive

The peer-to-peer network itself. No central server decides which blocks count. The more bees run nodes, the harder the hive is to switch off.

  • Sound, predictable supply

  • Open source

Where each block reward goes

In every block, for the whole emission schedule. Each halving halves all four amounts together.

Of every block reward: 80% to the miner, 8% to Core Development, 4% to Grants & Ecosystem and 8% to the Community & Development Reserve. In era 0 that is 5.00, 0.50, 0.25 and 0.50 coins.

  • MinerPaid to the forager who found the block. 5.00 coins per block in era 0. 80%
  • Core DevelopmentFunds work on the node, indexer and wallet. 0.50 coins per block in era 0. 8%
  • Grants & EcosystemFunds tooling, documentation and community work. 0.25 coins per block in era 0. 4%
  • Community & Development ReserveSet aside for the community and for future development. Governed and spent under the published custody policy. 0.50 coins per block in era 0. 8%

Fixed in the genesis rules for the whole emission schedule. Paid block by block as part of each block reward — not a premine.

At every halving

The block reward halves and the 80 / 8 / 4 / 8 split stays exactly the same. The percentages are fixed in the genesis rules and cannot be changed.

Over the whole life of the chain that is 80% to miners, 8% to Core Development, 4% to Grants & Ecosystem and 8% to the Community & Development Reserve.

In the last eras integer rounding retires the small streams first: Grants & Ecosystem rounds to zero from era 25, Core Development and the Reserve from era 26, and the miner keeps the remainder.

The reserve

Paid block by block to its own predefined address, like the other allocations. How it is governed and spent is set out in the published custody policy, linked from the network page.

Roadmap

The money first. Then the things you do with it.

The order is fixed; the calendar follows the work.

  1. Live

    SWARM mainnet

    Public testnet 21 September 2026; mainnet live since 2 October 2026. The coin, the chain and the apps to run them. The genesis block holds no coins; after a 30-day closed start, public mining opens on 1 November 2026, 15:42 UTC.

    You can

    • hold, send and receive SWM, shielded or transparent, in SWARM Wallet (the current version is published for Windows, macOS and Linux; Android follows)
    • check the genesis hash and every block for yourself — the node verifies, it never trusts
  2. Now

    Growing the swarm

    More bees, more cities, more ways in. In development: signed macOS builds, the Google Play and App Store listings for the mobile wallet, a second seed node in another failure domain, custody tooling for the three published funds, and an independent security review of the code as it was launched.

    You can

    • browse the chain in the mainnet block explorer
    • tell us what breaks
  3. Next

    SWARM Market

    In development. An online marketplace where merchants list what they sell and buyers pay in SWM, shielded by default. The wallet checks every invoice before you confirm; the Market never holds your coins; fees are shown before you pay; order details never touch the chain. Non-custodial invoices come first — escrow, multisignature and dispute handling need their own protocol and operational review before any of it ships.

    You can

    • pay for real goods and services with SWM
    • sell to the whole swarm without a payment processor
    • keep what you bought between you and the seller
  4. Pre-release

    Privacy browser

    The goal: a browser with the SWARM wallet built in, where paying a site is one click with the same confirmation screen as the wallet, trackers are blocked, fingerprinting is reduced, nothing phones home, and each site gets only the permissions you give it. The first SWARM Browser pre-release for Windows is available now: Google’s services removed, privacy defaults switched on and the wallet in the toolbar. Paying sites, tracker blocking, phishing protection, rewards that pay and builds for macOS and Linux are not in it yet. Download SWARM Browser →

    You can

    • pay from the address bar
    • browse without being followed
    • give a site access to your wallet only when you choose
  5. Then

    Private messaging

    End-to-end encrypted messages between people who hold SWARM wallets, with payments inside the conversation. Your chat identity is separate from your spending key, relays see only what they need to deliver, and no message is ever written to the chain.

    You can

    • message and pay in one place
    • send an invoice in a chat
    • verify a contact once and be told if their key ever changes

Shipped work carries the date it shipped. Nothing that has not started gets one: each product is announced here when it is finished and reviewed, and not before.

FAQ

Straight answers.

Is SWARM live?

Yes. SWARM mainnet has been live since 2 October 2026. Earlier wallet versions (desktop 0.1.0-mainnet.9 and older, Android 0.2.0-mainnet.4) no longer connect to the network; the current version is in the Ecosystem. The genesis hash and the three destination addresses are on the network page, so you can check that the chain you join is the one that was announced.

The genesis block holds no spendable coins; every SWM that exists has been mined.

What is SWM worth?

Whatever people agree it is worth. SWARM does not promise a price. Like any cryptocurrency, SWM can lose value, including all of it. Nothing on this site is financial advice.

Can I mine SWARM?

Not yet. SWARM is in a closed start: until 31 October 2026, 15:42 UTC only the project’s own machines mine, and for the 24 hours after that the people on the waiting list can mine too. From 1 November 2026, 15:42 UTC mining is open to everyone, and the node software will be published on this site. The proof of work is Equihash 200,9.

There is no promise that home computers stay competitive forever. As the network grows and difficulty rises, professional and specialised miners can join — and nothing in the rules keeps anyone out, at either end. Equihash hardware already exists, so that moment can come early. It is a consequence of keeping the rules open, and we say so rather than pretend otherwise.

Is there a premine or a head start?

The genesis block contains no spendable coins. But the start is not open to everyone. The start is closed for 30 days. Until 31 October 2026, 15:42 UTC only the project’s own machines can mine: in those 29 days about 33,408 blocks are produced, about 208,800 SWM (80% to the project’s mining wallet, 20% to the three funds), about 0.99% of the cap. From then until 1 November 2026, 15:42 UTC the people on the waiting list can mine too; from that moment mining is open to everyone and the node software will be published on this site.

Why: an orderly start. Liquidity pools and the infrastructure around the coin need coins before trading and public mining begin, and the project has a responsibility to the people who back it. The coins mined in the closed start are what the project uses for that. The aim is a market that starts stable and predictable rather than chaotic; it is an aim, not a promise about the price.

Every coin, those included, has to be mined, and transparent mining rewards mature after 100 blocks before they can be spent.

How did SWARM mainnet start?

SWARM mainnet has been live since 2 October 2026: its genesis block has the header time 15:41:37 UTC, and block 1 followed at 15:43 UTC. The genesis block contains no spendable coins, so nobody started with a balance. The first 30 days are a closed start: 29 days in which only the project mines, then 24 hours in which the people on the waiting list can mine too; from 1 November 2026, 15:42 UTC mining is open to everyone. The genesis hash and the three destination addresses are published on the verify page, so anyone can check that the chain they are syncing is the one that was announced.

The 20% of each block reward that goes to the three project destinations is not a premine and not hidden: it is paid block by block, in public, for the whole life of the chain, and the percentages cannot be changed.

Where do block rewards go?

Every block reward is split 80% to the miner who found the block, 8% to Core Development, 4% to Grants & Ecosystem and 8% to the Community & Development Reserve. That split runs for the whole emission schedule: each halving reduces all four amounts together and the structure never changes.

Each allocation is paid automatically to its predefined destination address. Destinations can only be changed through a formal protocol upgrade; the percentages themselves cannot be changed.

In the last eras integer rounding retires the small streams first: Grants & Ecosystem rounds to zero from era 25, Core Development and the Reserve from era 26, and the miner keeps the remainder.

No hidden treasury — every allocation is visible in every block. The full numbers are on the network page.

Who controls the three project destinations?

The project does, under the custody policy published with the launch manifest: keys generated offline, held on separate devices under a threshold policy, with the spending and recovery procedure rehearsed before the addresses were fixed into the rules. It is custody of the project’s share, not a governance vote: nothing can change the percentages.

How private is it?

Privacy is a choice you make per payment. Shielded transactions keep sender, receiver and amount encrypted on-chain, using zero-knowledge proofs. Transparent transactions do not, and the wallet tells you which kind you are about to make.

Shielding protects what is written to the chain. It does not protect everything about how you use the network — for example, your own network connection is a separate matter.

What is coming after the coin?

SWARM Market, an online marketplace where you pay merchants in SWM; a privacy browser with the wallet built in; and private, end-to-end encrypted messaging with payments inside the conversation. In that order, each when it is finished and reviewed. A first pre-release of SWARM Browser for Windows is already in the Ecosystem. The roadmap has the detail.

How do I reach the project?

Write to swarmofficial@atomicmail.io or follow @swarm_coin on X (opens in a new tab). Together with this site and our GitHub, those are the only official channels. Anything else claiming to be SWARM is not us.

We will never ask for your recovery words, private keys or a payment — not by email, not on X, not anywhere.
